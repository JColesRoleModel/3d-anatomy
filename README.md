# Tree of Life: Neurological Research Institute

An interactive 3D journey through the human body and its occult anatomy, set in a living night sky. Filter by body system, tap any structure to see its name, and install it on your phone as an app.

**Open it:** https://jcolesrolemodel.github.io/3d-anatomy/ (lands on the Home page, which asks where to go; the 3D scene itself is at `?view=3d`)

## Add it to your phone
- **iPhone (Safari):** open the link, tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the link, tap the ⋮ menu, then **Install app** or **Add to Home screen**.

Each system downloads the first time you turn it on and then works offline.

## The sky and the art
`cosmos.js` builds the stage around the body. The night sky (aurora and nebula after the two album covers Brian shared, a milky way and about 60,000 twinkling stars) breathes on a nine-second cycle. Shooting stars cross it every few seconds, and every 16 to 42 seconds a random event happens: a meteor shower, a lightning storm that lights up the pyramid, a comet, a nova or an aurora surge.

Around the body stand the pieces of the *Secret of the Ages* poster in 3D: the rose and blue Ionic pillars with the violet middle pillar, the emerald cross, the golden Tree of Life, the crown of rays, the star of David as a star tetrahedron, a zodiac wheel with the planets in their home signs, an orbiting zodiac ring, the Aquarian jar pouring light, the bag of magic dust, the pyramid with its shining capstone, and Saturn with its hexagon storm. The emerald tablet and the caduceus drift through the background. Everything is drawn in code, none of it can be tapped, and the pieces near the body fade when you zoom in. The ✦ button turns the sky, the art or the motion off, and can call up a sky event; motion starts off for people who ask their device to reduce motion.

The occult symbols breathe with the sky and light flows up the nadis.

The skin layer is smoothed like a mannequin at the groin (`tools/modesty.mjs`); the organs layer keeps the anatomical genitals.

## Systems and layers
Skeletal, muscular, fascia, circulatory, lymphatic, nervous, endocrine, sinuses, organs, skin, and joints and ligaments. A structure can belong to more than one system (the thymus, for example, is lymphatic and endocrine).

Each system has its own opacity slider and sub-filters, for example:
- **Skeletal:** skull, teeth, cervical, thoracic and lumbar spine, sacrum and coccyx, ribs, arms, pelvis, legs
- **Muscular:** depth (superficial, intermediate, deep) and body region
- **Nervous:** brain, spinal cord, cranial nerves (each nerve I–XII separately), spinal nerves, eye and tear ducts, ear
- **Endocrine:** each gland

Tap a part, then tap any group in its info card (like *Cranial nerves* or *Lumbar vertebrae*) to focus on the whole group while everything else fades.

Muscle depth isn't labelled in the source models. `tools/muscle-depth.mjs` measures it by checking, for points all over each muscle, how many other muscles sit between that point and the skin. Textbook groupings from the source (such as "Deep part of posterior compartment of leg") take priority, and `tools/catalog.mjs` has a short list of manual corrections.

## Occult anatomy
Seven overlay layers built from Brian's Canva boards (OCCULT ANATOMY OF MAN whiteboard, UNIVERSAL MAP, 7 CHAKRAS): chakras, elements and prana vayus, the pineal / pituitary / thalamus trinity, the hand map and mudras, the Tree of Life, nadis and kundalini, and electric / magnetic polarity. Tap any symbol for its card. Values the boards mark as unsure (?, ???), leave blank, or contradict each other on show in amber with an asterisk and a note.

The polarity layer also lists what Franz Bardon's *Initiation into Hermetics* says about man, woman and handedness, with the two places it differs from the board (the inside of the hands, and the feet, which the board leaves out).

The layer data lives in `data/occult.json`, generated by `node tools/occult.mjs` (it reads the models to place each symbol on the body). Edit the tables in that script, not the JSON.

## Path tracing

The info card for an artery, vein or nerve can trace it back to the heart, brainstem or spinal cord, out to all its branches, or along the route to a second part (`trace.js`). The branching lives in `data/paths.json`, generated by `node tools/paths.mjs`. Most links come from the nesting in the original Z-Anatomy files (which `build.mjs` flattens away, so the script reads the raw files); the heart, the brachial, lumbar and sacral plexuses, the brainstem nuclei, the sinuses and a few corrections are written by hand in that script. Every link is checked against the 3D models, and joins farther apart than 4 mm are drawn as dotted lines. The model has no separate lateral and medial cords, lumbar plexus or sacral plexus, so those are stand-in points.

## Conditions: scapular winging

Layers > Conditions opens a study card for scapular winging (`conditions.js`, text and sources in `data/conditions.json`). Each type (medial, lateral, rhomboid, secondary, pseudo, plus normal for comparison) shows the weak muscle, its nerve traced back to the spinal cord, numbered spots where that nerve is commonly injured, and effects with evidence badges. The arm can be raised forward or to the side, with a slider for how weak the muscle is.

The movement is illustrative. The collarbone, shoulder blade and arm move as rigid bones on a normal shoulder rhythm (Ludewig et al.), changed by made-up but plausible amounts for each type; everything else around the shoulder is bent to follow them by blending between the nearest bones. Only the right side moves.

## Site menu and account

`hub/nav.js` and `hub/nav.css` draw the same top bar on every page: the menu button, the logo with the page name, the page's own tools, and the account button. The menu slides out from the left with the page's tools, the practice routines, and every part of the site at the bottom. A page adds `<header id="hub-nav" data-section="…" data-base="…">` and calls `initHub({ tools })`. New parts of the site go in `SECTIONS` in `hub/nav.js` (3D Anatomy, Practice, Watch, Scroll, Tasks, Rings, Dashboard). The account (Firebase project `tree-of-life-nri`, `account.js`) is one sign-in for the whole site: saved views live under `users/<uid>/saves`, practice records under `users/<uid>/sessions`, and tasks under `users/<uid>/tasks` (see `firestore.rules`).

## Tasks

`tasks/` is the to-do list that used to be DoubleDoIt (doubledoit.web.app), moved in so everything shares one account. Two columns: **Due**, anything with a date, grouped Overdue / Today / Tomorrow / This week / Later; and **General**, sortable and shuffleable. A quick-add bar has General / Today / Tomorrow / Pick a date chips. Tasks live under `users/<uid>/tasks`, sync live between devices, and work offline. The top bar's tools add a task, export or import JSON (DoubleDoIt's export files import as-is), and clear done tasks. The background can be the night sky or the landscape picture, with a dimness slider, set per device. `tasks/logic.js` holds the date and sorting rules, with no page or Firebase code.

## Watch, Scroll, Rings and Dashboard

Everything a person does counts toward three daily rings: **Body** (yoga, stretching, lymph drainage, innervation, mudras), **Mind** (talks, books, the Scroll) and **Spirit** (meditation, breathwork, neuro-ocular). `hub/rings.js` holds the rings, the default goals (30 / 30 / 15 minutes), the practice-section-to-ring map, and `SessionTimer`, which times one session and rewrites a single `users/<uid>/activity` document every 20 seconds and on every stop. It only counts while running and while the page is showing, so time is kept if the page closes and never counted twice. Practice records (`users/<uid>/sessions` and the device's own copy) feed the rings too, so the practice stats and the rings always agree.

- `watch/`: the video library (`users/<uid>/library`, one document per YouTube video). It filters by ring, plays inside the page through the YouTube player, and counts only seconds actually playing. Long videos (45 min or more) resume where they stopped. 🎲 Random picks by ring and length, favoring what hasn't been played lately, and can also launch a practice routine (Body: innervation; Spirit: breath and neuro-ocular) or the Scroll (Mind). `watch/seed.json` is the starter library, loaded by itself the first time an account's library is empty (remembered in `prefs/main.starterWatch`).
- `scroll/`: a positive doomscroll (`users/<uid>/scroll`, one document per card). Time counts toward Mind while the page shows and you're active (it pauses after a minute idle). Each card keeps views, time on screen, liked and saved, added with Firestore increments. Stats show the cards and authors that held attention. `scroll/seed.json` holds the 156 starter cards, loaded by itself the first time (`prefs/main.starterScroll`). The clock starts when you start scrolling, and cards keep loading ahead of you.
- `rings/`: the one place for every stat (the practice pages' Stats buttons open it at `#practice`). Today's rings and streaks; any stretch of time (this week, a month, all time, or two dates) with totals, a trend chart, where the time went (practice by section and routine, each video, the Scroll), and every session with its start, end and length, practice down to each movement. Then the last 12 weeks and the goals (`users/<uid>/prefs/main`). **＋ Log time** (under today's rings) adds time done away from the site: what, ring, date, start and end (an end before the start means past midnight), tags and notes. It's saved as an `activity` document with source `manual`, so it counts everywhere, and opens from the session list to edit or delete.
- `dashboard/`: a screen to leave on, made for an older iPad (iOS 15) that can't run the 3D scene. It's its own installable app, with its own `manifest.webmanifest` and a small offline cache (`dashboard/sw.js`) that never downloads the 3D engine. It reads only the last 8 weeks, uses no blur or looping animation, and shows the clock, the temperature with its place (Open-Meteo, plus BigDataCloud's free lookup for the place name), rings and streak with this week's seven small rings, today's tasks, a card of the day from the Scroll, and the last five things completed. The top bar hides itself, and it goes to a dim red clock from 10pm to 6am.
- `home/`: the front door. `index.html` sends visitors here (before any 3D code loads) unless the address has `?view=`; the installed app starts here too.

`hub/page.css` and `hub/page.js` hold the look and the small helpers (sign-in gate, toast, dialogs) these pages share. The site menu is a slide-out drawer on every page: this page's tools, the practice routines, then every part of the site.

## Wellness practice

`wellness/` is the Tree of Life NRI 1.1 practice app (innervation routines, neuro-ocular eye work, breathwork, mudras and practice stats), copied from JColesRoleModel/TreeofLifeNRI1.1 (which stays as it was) and served at `/3d-anatomy/wellness/`. It is restyled to match the 3D site (`wellness/css/hub.css`, loaded last on every page): the same top bar and tree logo, and a bottom row of section chips with 3D Anatomy pinned at the left. Both sites share one installable app (`manifest.webmanifest`) and the lightning tree icon. It keeps its own service worker; its stats backup now uses the site's account, uploading each movement as it's recorded. During the rest between movements, gazes or mudras, the screen shows the one coming up ("Up next"), and the rest defaults to 5 seconds. Breathwork also has Open breathing: an open-ended timer with start and stop, no pattern or countdown, saved every minute as "Open breathing" (Spirit). Its Stats buttons open the Rings page. Each app's service worker only clears its own caches.

## How it's built
- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with [three.js](https://threejs.org) (bundled in `vendor/`). No build step: GitHub Pages serves the files as they are.
- `models/*.glb`: one model per source file, simplified and compressed for phones.
- `data/catalog.json`: every structure with its system tags and anatomical grouping. This is where study notes and extra layers will go.
- `tools/`: the scripts that converted the source models (see below).

### Rebuilding the models
The source models are the Z-Anatomy FBX files from https://github.com/LluisV/Z-Anatomy (`Resources/Models/FBX`).
```
npm install   # the tools' packages, pinned in package.json
node_modules/fbx2gltf/bin/<OS>/FBX2glTF --binary --input SkeletalSystem100.fbx --output raw/skeletal
node tools/build.mjs raw/skeletal.glb out/skeletal.glb skeletal 0.3   # also writes out/skeletal.json; copy it to tools/meta/
node tools/muscle-depth.mjs out/regions.glb out/muscular.glb data/catalog.json tools/meta/muscle-depth.json
node tools/modesty.mjs        # once the regions model is in models/: smooths the groin of the skin layer
node tools/catalog.mjs data/catalog.json
```
`tools/meta/` holds the structure lists that `build.mjs` writes next to each model.

### Accounts
Saved views sync to a person's account through Firebase (project `tree-of-life-nri`). `firebase-config.js` holds the web app config, `firestore.rules` the security rules (publish them with `firebase deploy --only firestore:rules`, or paste them into the Firebase console under Firestore > Rules), and `vendor/firebase.js` the SDK, bundled by `node tools/firebase-bundle.mjs`. The bundle includes the full Firestore client, with its on-device cache and live listeners, because Tasks needs them. It only loads once someone signs in.
